Professional Experience

  • Nov 2021 to Dec 2024

    Mechanical Engineer

    Nepal Electricity Authority, Ministry of Energy, Water Resources and Irrigation, Nepal

    After passing the competitive examination conducted by the Public Service Commission of Nepal, I joined the Nepal Electricity Authority as a Mechanical Engineer in November 2021 (2078 BS). It was one of the most sought-after engineering positions in the country at the time.

    My work was centered on power generation, and I served at several hydropower and electrical plants across Nepal. I was responsible for hydromechanical systems and for preventive and corrective maintenance of both hydromechanical and electromechanical components. Beyond maintenance, I took part in plant operation, energy and demand forecasting, and the scheduling and management of staff and their workload. Along the way, I gained exposure to transmission, distribution, and energy trading.

    Working in Nepal's hydropower sector meant dealing with challenges specific to the Himalayas. Most plants in Nepal are run-of-river, and the river water carries fine sand and abrasive sediment that erodes turbines and other hydromechanical parts. This erosion was one of the major issues in our plants and required yearly maintenance with the plant shut down, which caused significant financial losses and made it harder to balance the load. River discharge is also highly seasonal. In the dry season it can drop to about one-third, leading to power shortages, while in the monsoon it rises so high that surplus energy is spilled. We had to decide which turbines to run, and whether to run them at full or partial load, to balance market demand while keeping financial losses to a minimum. Because the power purchase agreements with private producers were on a take-or-pay basis, payment was due even when there was no market for the energy, which made balancing supply, demand, and cost a highly technical task.

    This role strengthened both my technical and managerial skills. It gave me a clear understanding of the dual role of an engineer and a manager in a power plant, built my confidence to work in demanding environments, and deepened my understanding of how energy markets operate in Nepal and around the world. During this time, I completed NEA induction training (December 2021), training in the operation and maintenance of hydropower equipment (February 2022), and e-GP training in public procurement (March 2022).

Publications

Peer-reviewed research from my master's study at Thapathali Campus, IOE.

Journal article · 2022

Simulation of Propeller Runner for Cylindrical Basin of Gravitational Water Vortex Power Plant

Shankar Nath Pandey, Raj Kumar Chaulagain, and Bimal Pandey

Advances in Engineering and Technology: An International Journal, Vol. 2, No. 1, pp. 87–101, December 2022

Gravitational water vortex power plants generate electricity from a water vortex at very low heads, roughly 0.7 to 2 m, and need little maintenance. In this study, we designed a new propeller-type runner and a matching cylindrical basin, and used CFD-based optimization to tune the blade angle, basin outlet diameter, runner position, and rotational speed. The optimized design, with a 43° blade angle, a 175 mm outlet (20% of the basin diameter), and the runner at about 65% of the basin height, reached an efficiency of about 23.6% at 40 rpm.
